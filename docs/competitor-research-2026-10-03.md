# FindPhotosOfMe: competitor evidence

Research retrieved 3 October 2026. Scope: event photo delivery and selfie search, with organizer/photographer payment and browser access for attendees. Public primary sources only. Prices below are advertised prices, not tested checkout totals; taxes, promotions, geographic pricing, and contract terms can change them. Feature statements describe vendor claims unless explicit customer-side evidence is supplied. No competitor revenue or profitability was verified.

## Main finding

This is an established commercial category with named real deployments, but the proposed combination of selfie search, browser access, and per-event payment already has close competitors. A viable entry therefore depends on a specific reachable buyer, dependable delivery, and a reason to choose this workflow. “No subscription” is a useful buying preference, not a unique selling proposition.

## Adjacent substitutes: photo collection rather than verified selfie search

**Kululu** sells a browser-based QR photo collection experience, live photo wall and digital album, with one-time Plus/Pro upgrades and separate business plans. Its public pricing text recommends Plus for up to 500 uploaded photos; live prices are inside a JavaScript embed that was not readable in this research. No primary evidence of selfie face-search capability was found in the pages inspected. This is a competing event-photo budget, not evidence that the products perform the same job. [Pricing](https://www.kululu.com/pricing), [plan mechanics](https://help.kululu.com/en/articles/12673225-how-do-kululu-plans-work).

**GUESTPIX** similarly emphasizes collecting guest photos/videos via QR, no guest registration, slideshows and full-resolution downloads. Consumer packages are one-time with 12-month hosting; business packages include moderation, branding and invitations. Exact prices were dynamically omitted from retrieved text, so none are asserted here. The business “unlimited guests” claim has a **1,000-guests-per-gallery fair-use limit**. No face-search feature was verified on inspected pages. [Consumer pricing and workflow](https://guestpix.com/pricing-/), [business plans](https://guestpix.com/business-pricing/).

## Evidence of actual use, and what it does not prove

1. **Kwikpic, customer-side corroboration.** Diabetes Research and Welfare Association publishes a Kwikpic invitation for its 13th Annual Conference, held 30–31 May 2026, with both website and mobile-app access. TRANSMEDCON 2025 independently publishes a Kwikpic face-recognition photo link on its official conference site. These are concrete evidence of organizer adoption, but neither discloses purchase amount, attendee conversion, or repeat use. [DRWA photo gallery](https://www.drwa.in/gallery/image-gallery), [TRANSMEDCON photos](https://www.transmedcon2025.com/photos).

2. **SpotMyPhotos, photographer-side corroboration.** Andy Barker Photography describes using SpotMyPhotos at a Wellington corporate conference, with selfie check-in and delivery while the event was running. The photographer reports guests posting on LinkedIn and social media. This is a first-party account from an operator using the service; social impact is self-reported, with no measured lift or control group. [Operator’s account](https://www.andybarkerphotography.co.nz/blog/event-photography-case-study-how-we-captured-a-wellington-corporate-conference/).

3. **SpotMyPhotos, vendor-hosted case study.** Named photographer Vail Fucci reports time savings, referrals and higher revenue. The vendor quotes eight hours saved per event and a 250% revenue increase since adoption. Treat these as a selected testimonial, not a causal estimate or a forecast for FindPhotosOfMe. The story also reveals a distribution path: an industry conference introduction, a first live event, then an ambassador relationship and peer referrals. [Case study](https://www.spotmyphotos.com/case-studies/vail-fucci-photography/).

4. **Pic-Time, vendor-hosted testimonials.** The official AI page names Molly Grunewald, Jeff and Cat, and Lindsay Coulter. Lindsay reports increased print sales after the feature launched. This supports a plausible buyer benefit beyond convenience but is not independently audited revenue evidence. [AI gallery delivery](https://pages.pic-time.com/ai-for-gallery-delivery).

5. **Waldo, vendor-hosted testimonial.** Goodwin Photography’s Krisna Goodwin reports that automatic photo finding replaced lengthy contestant sorting. Useful evidence of the job being addressed; no independently measured time saving, price paid, or vendor revenue is established. [Waldo events](https://waldophotos.com/events/).

6. **Usage counts require careful interpretation.** Google Play lists Kwikpic at **1M+ downloads**, a distribution indicator, not paying accounts or active organizers. GUESTPIX claims 150,000+ global events on its own site; this remains an unaudited vendor claim and concerns adjacent photo collection. [Google Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.kwicpic), [GUESTPIX](https://guestpix.com/pricing-/).

## Implications for FindPhotosOfMe — analysis, not established facts

- **There is real category demand.** Multiple named organizers and operators publish actual deployments. Evidence does not yet establish demand for another general-purpose entrant or the profitability of any vendor.
- **Prioritize the buyer’s repeated job.** Attendees receive the delight; photographers and recurring organizers control upload, distribution and purchasing. A small portfolio of repeat photographers could be more useful than many isolated attendees, but this needs paid-pilot validation.
- **Do not rely on face recognition as the moat.** It is offered by both specialist vendors and an incumbent gallery suite. Per-event payment and no guest app are already common. A defensible initial advantage could instead be a specific underserved workflow, trusted local relationships, or materially better completion rates.
- **Use buyer outcomes in interviews and copy.** Test reduced “where are my photos?” support, faster delivery, sponsor/organizer visibility, and photographer referrals. Avoid claiming these benefits quantitatively before measuring them in real events.
- **Beware of bundle economics.** A repeat photographer compares a standalone per-event fee against a $50/month Pic-Time Advanced subscription containing other tools. A one-off organizer compares it with $39–$94 event offers. Premium pricing needs a demonstrated operational benefit or included service.
- **Retaining the existing gallery workflow may matter.** selfieseek explicitly markets itself as a layer over existing delivery tools. Before building integrations, ask prospects whether another upload is actually the barrier and observe their real workflow.
- **Distribution is part of the product.** The organizer must reliably put the search link in front of photographed attendees. A working matcher cannot compensate for an unshared link. On-site QR prompts, event email and the photographer’s delivery process deserve pilot measurement.

## Open questions before any price or growth commitment

The research did not benchmark recognition accuracy, support response times, actual privacy controls, throughput, checkout fees, customer acquisition cost, retention or gross margin. No accounts were created and no photos were uploaded to competitors. Validate the smallest paid workflow with real organizers before investing in broad integrations, enterprise features, or paid acquisition. A future pricing comparison should normalize event photo count, actual file size, retention duration, allowed searches, branding, attendee friction and available support rather than compare headline prices alone.
