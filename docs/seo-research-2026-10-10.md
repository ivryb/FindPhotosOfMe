# SEO research

Research run 10 October 2026 with OpenSEO (DataForSEO data), about 1,080 credits. Volumes are average monthly Google searches in the United States unless another country is named. KD is keyword difficulty from 0 to 100. Competitor traffic figures are DataForSEO's modelled estimates, not measured analytics.

## Main finding

SEO can bring a steady trickle of qualified visitors, but it will not be the main source of customers. Almost nobody searches for the solution itself: "face recognition photo sharing", "ai photo sharing" and "facial recognition event photography" each get about 10 US searches a month. Buyers search for the problem they have, and that demand sits in weddings and group sharing ("photo sharing app for wedding", "best way to share photos with a group"). Conference organizers barely search at all.

The closest competitor, SpotMyPhotos, gets an estimated 1,400 organic visits a month, almost all from people typing its name. Direct outreach to photographers and organizers ([growth evidence](growth-channel-evidence-2026-10-03.md)) remains the main channel. SEO is a cheap supplement that compounds, so a few well-chosen pages are worth starting now.

Photographers search for client-gallery platforms and comparisons between them. CPCs of $7–25 show those clicks are worth money, but volumes are small. FindPhotosOfMe doesn't replace a client gallery, so pages here should pitch selfie search as something added to a photographer's existing delivery. "smugmug find my photos" (590) is attendees of SmugMug-hosted events looking for SmugMug's own face search, which shows the feature is familiar to event-photo customers.

The "face recognition event photos" results are weak: an event check-in page, PhotoDeck's feature page, a Waldo blog post from 2023, and SnapSeek's blog. A good page would rank quickly, but almost nobody searches it.

### Attendees

Attendees search by event name, and only for big events. Mass-participation events spike right after they happen. "nyc marathon photos" averages 1,000 a month (peak 9,900), "boston marathon photos" 880 (peak 6,600), "spartan race photos" 1,000, "coachella photos" 720. Conferences barely register: "sxsw photos" 40, "ces photos" 30, "dreamforce photos" 10, "web summit photos" 10. Race photo companies (MarathonFoto, Marathon Photos Live) own the race results with bib-number search.

The brand phrase is a trap. Google reads "find photos of me" (20), "search photos by face" (320) and "face search" (33,100) as reverse face search across the internet, and every top result is PimEyes or a clone. Those visitors want to find their face on the web, not in an event gallery. Don't target these keywords.

### Personal use

"google photos face recognition" (880), "google photos search by face" (390), "facial recognition photo app" (140) and "face recognition photo organizer" (50) come from people who mostly already have face grouping in Google or Apple Photos. A page could catch photos that aren't in someone's library, such as a shared drive or a ZIP from a friend, but the demand is small.

### Competitor brands

US: GuestCam 2,900, Waldo Photos 2,400, SpotMyPhotos 720 (plus 720 for "spot my photos"), Kwikpic 590, Premagic 70. Searches for "[brand] alternative" are near zero for all of them.

India: Kwikpic 27,100, Samaro 6,600, Premagic 1,000, "ai photo sharing" 170, "face recognition photo sharing" 90. Face-matched photo delivery is an established, branded category there, mostly for weddings, at CPCs of a few cents. The UK mirrors the US at about a quarter of the volume.

## What competitors' traffic is made of

- **Waldo** (est. 104,000/month): about 90% is "waldo" and "picture of waldo", from people looking for Where's Waldo. Its useful traffic comes from public gallery pages ranking for "[camp name] photos"; `/@campallen` ranks first for "camp allen pictures".
- **GuestCam** (est. 25,000/month): about 65% is a free QR code generator. The rest is comparison pages (`/versus/kululu` gets about 580/month) and blog posts such as "best way to share photos with a group".
- **SpotMyPhotos** (est. 1,400/month): almost entirely its brand name. Its headshot-photographer page gets about 15.
- **SnapSeek** (est. 50/month): a blog about face recognition for weddings and events that ranks only for low-volume terms.

## Pages to add, in priority order

1. **Weddings page** targeting "photo sharing app for wedding", "share wedding photos with guests", "wedding photos of guests" and "best wedding photo sharing app": about 1,900 searches a month at KD 0–18. Pitch it as the one link where guests both add their photos and find themselves in the photographer's photos.
2. **Guide: the best way to share photos with a group or event**, targeting "best way to share photos with a group" (880, KD 12) and the "event photo sharing" variants. Compare the free options honestly (Google Photos shared albums, Drive folders, QR uploads) and show where selfie search helps: big galleries where people only want their own photos.
3. **Comparison pages** for SpotMyPhotos, Waldo, GuestCam and Kwikpic. Little direct search volume, but they convert people already comparing tools, they're what AI assistants cite, and GuestCam's Kululu comparison shows the format earns traffic.
4. **A roundup of face-recognition photo sharing apps** to own that thin category and earn the "face recognition event photos" long tail. Include competitors fairly; the listicles that rank today are all written by vendors.
5. **Public gallery pages.** They're already crawlable and titled "{event} photos", and each one's logo links home. Small conferences won't bring searchers, but races and festivals will. Adding event date and place in text, and a "make a gallery like this" link, would help.

Leave these for later: per-use-case pages for corporate events, parties and races (low volume each; useful mainly as outreach landing pages), photographer platform comparisons (valuable clicks, but off-product), and a free tool in GuestCam's style (high traffic, low intent).

## Technical basics

- findphotosofme.com isn't in the connected Search Console account, which lists only supercharged.work and listenly.io. Verify the domain and submit a sitemap; the site has no sitemap.xml yet.
- Secret-link galleries correctly send noindex and are excluded in robots.txt. Public subdomain galleries are indexable.
- Most of these result pages show a Google AI Overview first, which cuts clicks. That makes being mentioned in the lists that rank (pix.wedding's 13-app comparison, event.pics' database of 25 apps, wedding.studio's ranking) nearly as valuable as ranking yourself. Asking to be included is cheap.

## What to expect

These figures are assumptions, not data. The clusters above total about 4,000–5,000 US searches a month. Ranking in the top three for most of them within 6–12 months, with AI Overviews taking a share of clicks, suggests 300–1,000 relevant visits a month. At a 2–4% signup rate that's roughly 6–40 signups a month, mostly wedding hosts and photographers rather than conference organizers.

## Open question

The [landing brief](landing-page-brief.md) leads with conferences and community events, but search demand sits in weddings and group sharing. Either keep conferences for outreach and use weddings for search, or reconsider the lead audience. That's a positioning decision, not an SEO one.
